01 / THE SWAP

Three steps, and the chain decides the last one

Nothing completes on a tap. The swap closes when the contract says the USDT was locked for these terms and then claimed.

A hold invoice states the 32-byte payment hash the escrow will be locked against. The page checks the bech32 checksum, the payee's signature over the invoice, the amount, the network prefix and the expiry before either side is bound to it — and a BOLT12 offer is refused outright, because the invoice a wallet gets for an offer carries a fresh hash of its own and the USDT would lock against a secret no payment moves.

Then the USDT address, then Approve, which reads the escrow before anything is signed: endpoints alive and agreeing, the contract deployed, the record for this exact swapId. The id is sha256(hashlock, sender, receiver, token, amount, timelock) — the funding address is part of it, so copying the terms does not let a stranger lock them first and burn the id by refunding.

The window has a floor, a ceiling and a margin: no less than 30 minutes, no more than 7 days, and the Lightning invoice has to stay payable at least an hour past the window. A record past its timelock reads as window closed, not as locked.

Step 1 · the invoice

A payment hash or nothing

A BOLT11 hold invoice with the standard payment_hash field and a signature the page verifies itself, in the page, with no network. An lno1 offer is refused with the reason and a request for a hold invoice; a BOLT12 invoice is refused because this page has no verifier for its Schnorr signature.

Step 2 · the address

Checked against its own spelling

An Arbitrum address with exactly 40 hex characters, not the zero address, and a mixed-case one must match its EIP-55 spelling — one replaced character is refused instead of locking money against it. A USDT address that arrives in a partner's code is shown and has to be tapped in, never written silently.

Step 3 · approve

One tap, one operation

Every tap that can move money marks itself in flight and refuses the next one before anything is built or sent, so a second tap in the read's gap cannot start a second swap on the same money. The terms a tap built are dropped on refusal, and the secret never leaves the page.

02 / THE ESCROW

Two legs, one secret, and who can move first

The contract has no owner, no fee switch and no proxy. It is small enough to read in one sitting.

Contract · solc 0.8.26

Lock, claim, refund

Checks-effects-interactions, custom errors, a reentrancy guard on all three movers, a zero hashlock and a receiver that is the escrow itself refused at the door. Token movement goes through _safeTransfer and a balance-delta check, so a fee-on-transfer token or one that returns no bool cannot leave the escrow holding less than it promised.

Reads · quorum

One node is not a cross-check

Sepolia is read from sepolia-rollup.arbitrum.io/rpc and arbitrum-sepolia-rpc.publicnode.com; One from its two public endpoints. A state the money depends on is believed only when two answers agree, and a single answer is shown as one endpoint, no cross-check rather than passed off as a check.

chain.js · receipts and transactions compared field by field

Clock

The chain's time, not the phone's

The window is measured against the head's timestamp — the median of the endpoints that agree within five minutes — and re-read before I paid starts watching. Moving the phone's clock cannot open a window the chain has closed.

chain.js · a head a day off is treated as a mistake, not a clock

Price

Two quotes, ninety seconds

The BTC/USD rate is mempool.space and Coinbase asked at the same time: two answers within 2% are one live rate, two answers further apart are not a rate at all, and a quote older than 90 seconds is read again before a swap is approved or a code is written. The offline guess never prices a wallet swap.

price.js · the rate line names what it is

Hand-off · EIP-1193 / EIP-681

Signing stays in your wallet

With a wallet in the page the approve, lock, claim and refund calls are sent from it and the allowance is checked first. Without one, the exact call is copied as text or opened as an ethereum: link, and the transaction hash is pasted back — the page reads the receipt itself and still judges only what the escrow says.

wallet.js · the app holds no key and never asks for one

Terms · cleanswap: code

Terms, not a web address

A code is base64url JSON under the QR budget: both amounts, the window, the hashlock, the address, and in Wallet mode the chain and escrow. It never switches the network and never writes an escrow address — a code naming another escrow is refused whole, with the reason on screen.

03 / SCREENS

Practice and Wallet, the same three steps

A rehearsal walks the identical screen, gate and reading against a stand-in escrow, so what Practice teaches is what Wallet does.

Practice

Nothing to deploy, nothing written down

The stand-in escrow lives in the page and answers with the same record shape as the contract, at rehearsal speed. A practice swap is never written to Recent and never survives the page — its escrow died with it.

Wallet

A swap that outlives the page

An external wallet is another app, and Android may kill the WebView while it is in front. The terms of a running swap go into one localStorage key before that happens and are read back at boot — the terms, the window, the addresses and the transaction hashes, never the payment secret.

Shell

No screenshots, one document

The Android shell sets FLAG_SECURE, serves exactly one page from its own virtual https origin, keeps the WebView cache off, refuses plain HTTP except to loopback, and hands lightning:, bitcoin:, ethereum: and allowlisted https: links to the app that owns them.

05 / LIMITS

What it does not do

A tool that hides its edges cannot be audited — and this one moves money.

Unaudited

The escrow is minimal by design and has not been audited. Treat it as a prototype and rehearse in Practice on Sepolia before anything real.

It never signs

The page reads the chain and builds calls; a wallet signs them. Anything it reports as settled is read from swaps(swapId), and everything else is a sentence, not a receipt.

One endpoint is trusted

With a single RPC endpoint there is no cross-check, and the screen says so. Run your own node for real value.

BOLT12 is refused

An lni1 invoice carries a Schnorr signature this page cannot verify, so it is refused and an ordinary hold invoice is asked for.

Practice invoices cannot be paid

They are real BOLT11 strings signed by a key that ships in the page. They verify and they parse, and no node holds that key.

Two clocks, one order

The payment has to outlive the escrow. An invoice whose final delta would end before the window plus an hour is refused, because otherwise one secret could collect both legs.
