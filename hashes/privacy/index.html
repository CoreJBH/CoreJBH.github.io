Privacy Policy

App: Hashes — solo Bitcoin miner for Android · Package com.corejbh.hashes · Developer CoreJBH · Effective 28 November 2026

The short version

Hashes keeps the mining session on the phone. It has no account, no analytics and no server of its own, and it never asks for a private key or a seed phrase. The only connection it opens is the pool you chose, and the only thing it knows about you is the payout address you typed or scanned.

What leaves the phone

  • The pool you chose. Exactly one connection, to the Stratum V1 endpoint selected in POOL & PAYOUT — TLS where the pool offers it. The login line carries your payout address and the worker name <address>.hashes, because that is how a solo pool knows where to pay a block; the password field carries the pool's conventional x. What that pool then does with the address and your IP is the pool's policy, not this app's.
  • Nothing else. No telemetry, no crash reporting, no advertising SDK, no Google Play Services, and no request to a server the developer runs. The app has no account system to log in to.

What stays on the phone

  • The payout address you typed or scanned, the chosen pool endpoint and the TURBO preference, in the app's own preferences file.
  • Your best hashes — session and all-time — so the terminal can show the best work this device has produced across restarts.
  • Nothing to spend. The app holds no wallet, no key and no seed. A payout address is a destination, not a credential: a pool pays it in the coinbase of any block found, and the coins are spendable only with a wallet you control elsewhere.

Permissions

PermissionWhy
INTERNETThe Stratum connection to the pool you selected.
CAMERAOnly while the QR scanner is open, to read a payout address from a QR code. Never in the background, and no image is stored or sent.
FOREGROUND_SERVICEA running session keeps going while the app is not on screen; Android only allows that as a foreground service.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNCThe type that session runs under: an ongoing data connection, which is exactly what the pool link is.
POST_NOTIFICATIONSThe status-bar entry a foreground service must show: live hash rate, mode and a STOP action (Android 13+, asked at the first start).

The app does not ask for ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE: an unreachable pool simply fails the connection and the status line reports it. The manifest declares the camera as not required, so the app installs on a device without one and the address can still be typed or pasted.

Background

Mining continues when you leave the terminal, and the notification is how you can tell. The session runs as a dataSync foreground service: the notification shows TURBO while TURBO is on, the pool link, the live hash rate and the block being hashed, and carries a STOP action that ends it at once. Nothing else runs in the background — no scheduled work, no wake lock, no sensor.

It stops when you press [ START / STOP MINER ], when you press STOP in the notification, or when you swipe the app out of the recent-tasks list. If Android itself ends the foreground service, the session stops with it rather than lingering half-alive, and nothing reconnects on its own.

Declining the notification permission on Android 13+ does not stop the service: the session still runs, the status-bar entry is simply not drawn, and the running service stays visible in the system's own task manager.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes the saved address, the endpoint and the best-share records. Nothing is kept anywhere else, because there is nowhere else.
