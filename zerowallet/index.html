ZeroWallet

A Lightning and on-chain Bitcoin wallet that runs only on the phone. The node, the wallet and the keys are one app built on LDK Node — no account, no backend, no LSP, no custodian anywhere in the payment path. A channel is opened peer to peer, and the recovery phrase is entered in numbered boxes, not one long field.

01 / THE WALLET

Two balances, one screen, no server in between

The Lightning balance and the on-chain balance are read from the node on this phone, never from an API.

On-chain, the wallet derives a fresh address for every receive, sizes a send from the network's own fee estimate, or sweeps the whole balance. The confirmation screen prints the destination address in full monospace, wrapped over as many lines as it takes — never an abbreviation somebody could swap characters inside. Amounts are read as exact decimals: more than the 21 million supply, a sub-satoshi remainder or an overflow is refused instead of paid.

On Lightning it creates BOLT11 invoices and BOLT12 offers, pays BOLT11 invoices, BOLT12 offers and BOLT12 invoices, and reads the same QR for a BIP-21 request that carries both halves. A Lightning send reads sending until the node reports the payment settled, and only then sent: routing can still fail after the send screen is done with it, and the history says so instead of hiding the attempt.

Channels are opened peer to peer with a pubkey@host:port, closed cooperatively or force-closed. There is no just-in-time channel service and no channel-opening fee to a third party. The channel graph arrives from the node's peers, or as a Rapid Gossip Sync snapshot picked per chain.

Never ₿0 while it starts

The last real sync is kept

A start opens the node store and syncs with Esplora, which takes seconds. The last known balances, block height, sync time and payment list are sealed on the device after every real sync and shown while the node comes up, so opening the wallet never flashes a zero balance.

Fees

The rate on screen is the rate signed

Normal and Fast come from the chain source's estimator, parsed as JSON and clamped to 1..1000 sat/vB. The confirmation shows the rate and the estimated total together, and a rate of 100 sat/vB or more, or a fee above 10% of a 1,000-sat payment, stops the send behind its own confirmation.

Offline

A dash, not a stale number

Every fiat figure is the dollar value of the balance, from CoinGecko with mempool.space as the fallback. With no network the wallet prints a dash instead of yesterday's price, and the satoshi figures stay exactly as the node last reported them.

  • Native, not a WebView. Kotlin 2.0.21 and Jetpack Compose, minSdk 26, one app id com.corejbh.zerowallet.
  • A chain source that moves instead of dying. Mainnet starts on blockstream.info/api; when that endpoint times out the same start is retried on mempool.space/api and then mempool.emzy.de/api, and the screen names the server it is reading. A node that is already running walks the same list instead of sitting on “Sync problem”.
  • A port that cannot be bound is not a dead wallet. If 9735 is taken, the node starts again without a listening address, so on-chain sync, payments and open channels keep working.
  • One lock around every JNI call. Starts, stops, wipes, restores and chain switches are serialised, and a stop waits for the event loop to leave its native call before the node is destroyed.
  • Stops that stay stopped. A node the user stopped is written to disk, so a killed process does not bring it back; coming back to the wallet quietly resyncs one that survived.
  • Nothing in the notification bar. No foreground service, no ongoing notification: the node lives as long as the app does and reconnects when the screen returns.
03 / KEYS & RECOVERY

The phrase is stored as ciphertext a lock has to open

Every privileged action asks for the device first, and the answer is what runs the action.

Seed · Android Keystore

AES-256-GCM, generated in the Keystore

The key's material is created inside the Keystore and never exists as bytes this process can hold. On a phone with a screen lock it is created setUserAuthenticationRequired(true) with a 60-second window, so a shell, a hooked callback or a bypassed screen cannot read the phrase.

core/SecureStore.kt · fail-closed: a seal it cannot make is not written

Restore · BIP-39

One numbered box per word

12 or 24 words, three columns, one word per box. As soon as the letters name exactly one BIP-39 word the box fills itself and the caret moves on; ambiguous prefixes offer tappable suggestions. Pasting a whole phrase — spaces, newlines, commas or a numbered list — spreads it over the boxes.

core/Bip39.kt · official vectors in the unit tests

Backup · .zwbackup

PBKDF2, 210,000 iterations

The node store — channel manager and monitors, on-chain wallet, node keys — is packed into one file sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from the recovery phrase: PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 210,000 iterations, a random 16-byte salt in the header (format 3). Older format 2 and format 1 files still open.

core/BackupLayer.kt · Android's own file picker, no Play Services

Gate · BiometricPrompt

Send, show, restore, wipe

Sending, funding or closing a channel, restoring a backup, showing the phrase and wiping the wallet all ask for a strong biometric or the screen lock first. The prompt's answer is the only thing that runs the action: a cancel, a dismissal or a phone with no lock runs none of it.

core/AppLock.kt · Authorization.guarded()

Screen · FLAG_SECURE

No screenshots, no Recents thumbnail

The wallet window is closed to screenshots and screen recording and blanked in the recent-apps list, and the recovery phrase and the setup screens force it on. The phrase dialog is dropped on pause and on stop whether or not lock-on-open is set.

core/ScreenSecurity.kt · the pictures below come from a demo build

Clipboard

A copy that hides itself and expires

Copying an invoice, an offer, an address or a node id marks the clip EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE on Android 13+, so the system does not draw it in the clipboard preview. Two minutes later the app takes the copy back itself — a leftover address is what a clipboard-swapping app changes between the copy and the paste.

04 / INSTALL

Download, check, restore

One APK, one hash, no account. The release is signed with the project's own key, and the phrase is the only thing that carries a wallet from another build.

  • Download zerowallet-1.2.3-release.apk from Releases — v1.2.3, versionCode 24, 47,511,375 bytes.
  • Allow “install unknown apps” for the app you open it with, then install. Android 8.0 (API 26) or newer, arm64-v8a or armeabi-v7a.
  • Create a wallet on 12 or 24 words and write the phrase down. It is sealed with a key bound to the phone's lock, so removing that lock makes the stored copy unreadable — the words are the way back.
  • Coming from 1.1.x? That build was debug-signed and carries another package id, so it cannot be updated in place. Write the recovery phrase down, install 1.2.3, restore from the words or from a .zwbackup, and only then uninstall the old one.

Channel funds need an on-chain reserve of about 1% of the channel size plus the funding fee, and closing a channel costs an on-chain transaction. Keep channel amounts small while the peer-to-peer flows get real-world mileage.

05 / LIMITS

What it does not do

A wallet that hides its edges cannot be trusted with money.

The phrase restores coins, not channels

Open channels live in this phone's node store. Without a .zwbackup a lost phone loses the funds in them, and a backup is a snapshot — a channel that moved after it may force-close on restore.

The node store is not encrypted at rest

ldk-node 0.7.0 writes channel monitors and the BDK wallet as plain SQLite under the app's private directory. The sandbox, allowBackup="false" and file-based encryption protect it from other apps and from a locked phone; a rooted or unlocked phone hands over the channel balance.

Not a hosted wallet

Nobody recovers a channel for you and no service keeps a copy. Backups exist when you save one, from the phone, yourself.

Inbound Lightning needs reachability

A phone behind carrier NAT cannot accept inbound connections without port forwarding, which is most of what BOLT12 and receiving a first channel needs.

Testnet3, not Testnet4

ldk-node 0.7.0 exposes Testnet3; the wallet offers mainnet, signet and testnet3 only.

A wallet at 1.2.3

Self-custody software under active development: keep only amounts you are willing to lose while the peer-to-peer flows get mileage.
